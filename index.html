CHAPTER 01 · CORE CONCEPT

Meaning and Significance of Political Theory

Meaning

Political theory is the systematic study of political ideas, concepts, values, institutions and practices. It asks how political life works, why it works in a particular way, and how it ought to be organised.

Remember: Political theory does not merely describe political reality. It also evaluates, questions and criticises it.

Three dimensions

Normative — What ought to be?
Examines values and ideals such as justice, liberty, equality and rights.

Empirical — What is?
Studies observable political behaviour, institutions and realities.

Analytical / Conceptual — What does it mean?
Clarifies political concepts and examines their relationships.

Significance

  1. Conceptual clarity: explains liberty, equality, justice and democracy.
  2. Evaluation: tests whether laws and institutions are fair and legitimate.
  3. Guidance: provides principles for reform and public policy.
  4. Critical thinking: challenges assumptions, authority and injustice.
  5. Theory and practice: connects political ideals with real institutions.

Thinkers to remember

Plato: justice and the ideal state.

Aristotle: constitutions and the good life.

Karl Marx: class, ideology and material conditions.

John Rawls: justice as fairness.

Exam connection

This foundation supports the PSIR topics of state, liberty, equality, justice, rights, democracy, power and political ideologies.
